Chapter 2 · Fire

What it teaches. Combustion: air-fuel ratio, flame temperature and heat recovery. You build a burner, a boiler and a converter feed and keep the gas at the right temperature.

5 jobs: L05 to L09. Site: Burner house. L05 to L08 are drawn in the new look. L09 is in the classic look for now.

L05 Heat for Free New look

Sulfur burner house, waste-heat bay

Brief

  • That burner throws away a great deal of heat. Make steam with it, forty bar (the HP setting of the boiler: LP is 4 bar, MP 10, HP 40), and keep the gas to the converter at 420 degrees.
  • Then hold it for a three-minute shift. The blower wears and the feed surges; the air is your dial: the flow knob on the air feed F-102 (the blower only lifts the pressure). A feed surge raises the sulfur and the heat with it, so SO₂ and the heat ledger read higher after the shift than at your design point.
  • What to build: sulfur feed and blower air into a mixer, then burner, waste-heat boiler, stack; set the boiler outlet to 420 °C. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; the printout marks your call right or wrong.

Goal

  • Export at least 7 t/h of 40 bar steam
  • Burner gas 9.5 to 11 % SO₂
  • Gas to the converter at 420 °C, within 5 °C
  • Hold the plant through the 180 s shift

Stars

  1. Goals met at the end of the 3-minute shift
  2. No High alarm for more than 60 s
  3. Net steam value minus blower cost at least 222 USD an hour (3 % better than the reference design)

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • The gas reaches the converter too cold.
  • Not enough steam.
  • The gas reaches the converter too hot.
  • The burner gas is too rich.
  • The burner gas is too weak.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Start the burner house: put the air blower on the plot, beside the air feed.sulfur burns with whatever air it gets, so the air flow (the air feed F-102 knob) is your dial for the whole level.
  2. Add the mixer: the sulfur feed and the blown air meet here.the burner needs one stream with fuel and oxygen in the right proportion.
  3. Add the burner after the mixer.this is where the 930-degree gas and all the free heat come from.
  4. Put the waste-heat boiler after the burner, between the burner and the stack.the burner gas leaves near 930 °C; the boiler turns that heat into steam instead of letting it go up the stack.
  5. Add the stack at the end: the gas leaves the boiler here for the converter.the stack stands for the converter inlet; its temperature and SO₂ are what you are graded on.
  6. Pipe it in order: sulfur feed to the mixer, air feed to the blower, blower to the mixer, then mixer, burner, boiler, stack.click an outlet, then the inlet it feeds; the gas has to take this path to be cooled before the stack.
  7. Set the boiler gas outlet to 420 °C and keep the steam level on HP.the converter catalyst lights off near 420 °C. Colder gas, more steam, but the converter will not start.
  8. Turn the key to commission.read the boiler duty and the steam: duty is steam times 2361 kJ per kg.
  9. Start the shift.a plant that is right at noon can be wrong at three. The shift draws its events from the seed.
  10. When the blower wears or the feed surges, give the air feed a new flow.sulfur burns with whatever air it gets; less air makes richer, hotter gas, more air weaker gas.

L06 Too Hot to Hold New look

Sulfur burner house, the lining

Brief

  • The burner lining is rated 1100 °C. Richer gas means a smaller converter, but do not cook the brickwork.
  • The converter still needs oxygen: at least 0.8 mol of O₂ for every mol of SO₂. Air that goes round the burner is cold air.
  • What to build: the L05 burner house (blower, mixer, burner, boiler, stack) with the air set for richer gas, plus a splitter and a second mixer that bring dilution air round the burner. You need both: one air stream cannot make the burner hot enough and the converter gas lean enough at the same time. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; the printout marks your call right or wrong.

Goal

  • Burner gas at least 11 % SO₂
  • Burner gas no hotter than 1,100 °C
  • Gas to the converter at least 0.8 mol O₂ per mol SO₂
  • Hold the plant through the 600 s shift

Stars

  1. Goals met at the end of the shift
  2. No lining damage: never at the 1100 °C alarm in the shift
  3. Burner gas at least 11.8 % SO₂ (3 % richer than the reference; the dilution air makes it possible)

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • The burner gas is above the lining rating.
  • The burner gas is not rich enough.
  • The converter gas is short of oxygen.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Build the burner house first: put the air blower on the plot, beside the air feed.the air flow on the air feed F-102 is your dial for how rich the gas is.
  2. Add the mixer where the sulfur feed and the blown air meet.the burner needs one stream with fuel and oxygen together.
  3. Add the burner after the mixer.the burner lining is the limit of this level.
  4. Add the waste-heat boiler after the burner.it takes the heat out of the gas before the stack.
  5. Add the stack at the end: it stands for the converter inlet.its gas is what you are graded on.
  6. Pipe it in order: sulfur feed to the mixer, air feed to the blower, blower to the mixer, then mixer, burner, boiler, stack.click an outlet, then the inlet it feeds.
  7. With the house standing, push the SO₂ up by cutting the air. Read the burner gas temperature.the same heat in less gas makes hotter gas. Temperature rise is heat divided by heat capacity.
  8. Add the dilution air: a splitter after the blower, a second mixer after the burner.air that goes round the burner cannot cool the flame, but it brings the oxygen the converter needs.
  9. Start the shift and watch the lining alarm.blower wear takes air away and the gas gets richer and hotter. Add air before the lining alarm.

L07 Hess’s Road New look

Coke yard, the gasifier line

Brief

  • The coke furnace is down. We have a gasifier that turns coke to CO and a CO burner. The process heater still needs 10 megawatts.
  • Two steps, one coke pile. Add the heat of both, and mind the heat the gasifier gives off on the way. The process heater is the HEATER part from the tray, already set up as a heat sink with no utility: it takes the heat out of the hot gas and cools it to 25 °C, and its duty is the heat delivered. The COOLER in the tray is a water quench: think hard before you use it. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • Deliver 10 MW to the process heater, within 0.2 MW
  • Use both steps and burn all the CO: at most 0.5 kmol/h of CO to the stack

Stars

  1. Goals met: 10 MW through gasifier and CO burner
  2. Capture the gasifier heat too: at most 0.1 MW lost on the way
  3. Call it: the coke the job needs, kmol/h, within 1 %

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Heat is leaving the road.
  • The heater gets more heat than it needs.
  • Only one step ran.
  • The heater is short of heat.
  • CO is leaving by the stack.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Place the gasifier after the coke and air mixer.coke burns to CO first, and half an O₂ per carbon does it.
  2. Place the CO burner after the gasifier.the CO is a fuel with 283 kJ per mole still in it.
  3. Place the process heater and the stack at the end.the heater takes the heat out of the hot gas; what leaves is cold.
  4. Pipe it: coke and air to the mixer, then gasifier, CO burner, heater, stack.every outlet ends in a unit or the stack.
  5. Set the coke flow and the air, then commission.read the heat ledger: what each step released and what reached the heater.
  6. Read the heat ledger: gasifier, CO burner, heater.the two steps add to the heat of burning carbon straight to CO₂, whichever road you take.
  7. Ask Why? and follow the kilojoules.minus 110.5 and minus 283.0 make minus 393.5, which is carbon to CO₂ in one go.

L08 Pay Once New look

Furnace line, synthesis gas preheat

Brief

  • Our furnace line heats 1000 kmol/h of synthesis gas from 40 to 420 °C, and the old furnace throws its flue out at 900 °C. Fix that: buy the heat once.
  • Approach no tighter than 20 K, stack no cooler than 120 °C. The exchanger costs by the square metre; the fuel costs by the hour. The shift is 30 plant minutes: turn the speed key to 16x and it takes under two minutes of your time.

Goal

  • Synthesis gas to the reactor at 420 °C, within 1 °C
  • The exchanger covers at least 25 % of the heating duty
  • Hold the plant through the half-hour shift (30 min)

Stars

  1. Synthesis gas at 420 °C, heat recovered, and the shift held
  2. Both rules kept: approach at least 20 K and stack at least 120 °C
  3. Total cost (fuel plus annualised plant) no more than 78 USD an hour (within 3 % of the best area)

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Hardly any heat is recovered.
  • The synthesis gas is not at 420 °C.
  • The flue leaves too cold.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Place a heat exchanger: flue on the hot side, synthesis gas on the cold side.heat flows from the 900 °C flue to the 40 °C gas for nothing, if there is area to carry it.
  2. Pipe it: flue through the exchanger to the stack, gas through the exchanger, the fired heater and on to the reactor.the fired heater only trims what the exchanger could not do.
  3. Set the exchanger area and commission.area buys approach. Heat moved is the area times U times the temperature gap.
  4. Try a second area and compare the total cost.past some area each extra square metre saves less fuel than it costs.

L09 Exam Bridge: Bomb vs Burner Classic look

Combustion lab, the calorimeter bench

Brief

  • The bomb calorimeter and the flow calorimeter disagree by five kilojoules on methane. Neither is broken. Call the bomb reading before you run the bench.
  • Then the three exam items: the notebook has a row for each. Write every answer down before you run the bench, then see which were right. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; the printout marks your call right or wrong.

Goal

  • Run the bench: the flow calorimeter reading of the heat of burning CH₄ is pending (not run yet)
  • Predict the bomb reading (ΔU of burning CH₄, kJ/mol) to within 0.5 %: your prediction is pending

Stars

  1. The bomb prediction within 0.5 %
  2. Two of the three exam items right: the heat from the carbon, the energy of vaporising water, and the furnace switch temperature
  3. All three exam items right

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • You called the flow calorimeter, not the bomb.
  • No bomb prediction was made before the run.
  • The bomb prediction is off.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Call it first: the heat CH₄ gives in the bomb. Then make the three exam predictions.a prediction after the run is a reading, not a prediction.
  2. Commission the bench to run the experiments.the flow calorimeter holds the pressure, the bomb holds the volume.
  3. Read the two needles and the gap between them.when gas moles change, constant-volume heat differs from constant-pressure heat by dn times RT.
  4. Ask Why? and follow the moles of gas.two moles of gas disappear, so the atmosphere does work on the system at constant pressure.
