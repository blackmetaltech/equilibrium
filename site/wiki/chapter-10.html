Brief

  • The condensate return needs a new pump and line: … m³/h of … kg/m³ condensate at 90 °C from the vented collecting tank T-301 at grade to the deaerator D-302, … m up on the boiler-house deck and held at … bar g. Pick a pipe size for each leg, a pump from the catalogue and, if you like, trim its impeller.
  • Keep liquid velocities in the guide: 1 to 3 m/s in the discharge, at most 1.5 m/s in the suction. A small pipe is cheap to buy and dear to run: friction grows with the velocity squared and the motor pays for every metre of it. The pump runs where its curve crosses the system curve, not at its nameplate.

Steps

  1. Start with the discharge leg: pick the size that keeps … m³/h between 1 and 3 m/s.below 1 m/s you paid for steel you do not need; above 3 m/s friction and erosion take over.
  2. Now the suction leg: at most 1.5 m/s, usually one size up from the discharge.the suction loss comes straight off the NPSH available, and hot condensate has little to spare.
  3. Pick a pump whose curve crosses the system curve at or just past … m³/h, inside its catalogue range. Trim the impeller if it gives too much.the pump settles where the two curves cross. Extra flow is extra power, paid every hour of the year.
  4. Turn the key to commission the line.the run reads the operating point, the NPSH margin and the annual cost.
  5. Ask Why? and compare your annual cost with the best line.the economic pipe size balances steel against electricity.
