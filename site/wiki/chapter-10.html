Chapter 10 · Hydraulics

What it teaches. Pumps, pipe sizes, NPSH and cavitation. You size a line against the pump curve and pick the cheapest one over a year.

Size the Line

L39 Size the Line New look

Boiler house, condensate return

Brief

  • The condensate return needs a new pump and line: 60 m³/h of 965.3 kg/m³ condensate at 90 °C from the vented collecting tank T-301 at grade to the deaerator D-302, 14 m up on the boiler-house deck and held at 0.35 bar g. Pick a pipe size for each leg, a pump from the catalogue and, if you like, trim its impeller.
  • Keep liquid velocities in the guide: 1 to 3 m/s in the discharge, at most 1.5 m/s in the suction. A small pipe is cheap to buy and dear to run: friction grows with the velocity squared and the motor pays for every metre of it. The pump runs where its curve crosses the system curve, not at its nameplate.
  • Mind the suction. 90 °C condensate is only 3.29 m of head away from boiling at atmospheric pressure; the tank level gives 2 m more, and every metre lost in the suction line comes off the top. The pump can stand near the tank (6 m of suction) or at the foot of the boiler house (115 m of suction, 16 m of discharge). Electricity costs 0.1 USD per kWh here. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; the printout marks your call right or wrong.

Goal

  • Deliver at least 60 m³/h
  • Velocities in the guide: discharge 1 to 3 m/s, suction at most 1.5 m/s
  • NPSH margin of at least 1 m (NPSH available minus required)
  • The pump runs on its catalogue curve, between its minimum flow and the end of the curve

Stars

  1. Goal met: the deaerator gets its condensate without cavitation
  2. Annual cost (capital plus electricity) within 5 % of the economic optimum
  3. The pump runs at 70 to 110 % of its best-efficiency flow

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Over budget.
  • The pump cavitates.
  • Discharge too fast.
  • Short of the design flow.
  • Below minimum flow.
  • Off the end of the curve.
  • No flow.
  • Discharge oversized.
  • Suction too fast.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Now the suction leg: at most 1.5 m/s, usually one size up from the discharge.the suction loss comes straight off the NPSH available, and hot condensate has little to spare.
  2. Turn the key to commission the line.the run reads the operating point, the NPSH margin and the annual cost.
  3. Ask Why? and compare your annual cost with the best line.the economic pipe size balances steel against electricity.
