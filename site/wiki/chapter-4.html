L15 Rate vs Ceiling

Converter hall, the single bed

Brief

  • Now the iron catalyst at its measured rate, and the clock at full speed. One bed, a fixed load of catalyst. Find the inlet temperature that makes the most ammonia.
  • Too cold and the iron sleeps. Too hot and the ceiling drops. The answer is in between.

Goal

  • NH₃ at the bed outlet at least … mol %
  • No catalyst hotter than … °C
  • Catalyst load stays at … kg

Stars

  1. At least 10 mol % NH₃ at the outlet and no catalyst above 550 °C
  2. Bed stays inside the catalyst window: hottest point at most 520 °C, so the BED T HI lamp stays dark
  3. At least 10.6 mol % NH₃ at the outlet

Steps

  1. Put the feed preheater on the line. Its outlet temperature is your dial.the bed is adiabatic, so the inlet temperature decides where on the curve it works.
  2. Pipe it: feed to the preheater, preheater to the bed, bed to the tank.every outlet must end in a unit or a tank.
  3. Commission and watch the Equilibrium Window.the bed climbs from its inlet toward the ceiling; how far it gets is a race of rate against equilibrium.
  4. Read the window: the outlet against the equilibrium curve, and the approach.the approach is how much of the way to equilibrium the iron managed in the time it had.
  5. Change the inlet temperature and commission again. Try cooler, then hotter.the best temperature is the one where the rate and the ceiling trade off, and you find it by moving.
  6. Ask Why? and read the approach to equilibrium.it is built from your own bed numbers.

What it teaches

Rate against ceiling. Your gas entered at … °C and left at … °C with … % NH₃. The conversion reached … of the … equilibrium allows at that outlet temperature: an approach of … %. Colder is a higher ceiling but a slower bed; hotter is faster but a lower ceiling. Runs tried: ….

L16 Light-off

Contact bed one, acid plant

Brief

  • Below about four hundred degrees that catalyst is asleep. Above six-twenty it starts to die. Run bed one between.
  • A cold plant has no heat to make heat with. The start-up heater is dear; the exchanger is how you stop paying for it.

Goal

  • Bed-one SO₂ conversion at least … %
  • Hottest catalyst at most … °C
  • Bed catalyst as supplied (… kg): leave it alone

Stars

  1. At least 60 % converted with the bed under 620 °C
  2. Heater only trims: at most 10 kW of fuel. Let the hot bed effluent warm the cold feed: a bigger feed-effluent exchanger (about 50 m²).
  3. Cost per tonne at or under the calibrated figure

Steps

  1. Place the feed-effluent exchanger. The hot bed outlet will warm the cold feed.the bed gives off heat once it is lit; the exchanger hands it back to the gas still on its way in.
  2. Place the start-up heater after the exchanger. Its outlet temperature is the bed inlet.a cold plant has no hot effluent yet. Something has to light the catalyst first.
  3. Pipe the cold side: feed to the exchanger cold side (COLD IN), exchanger to the heater, heater to the bed.the bed outlet runs back through the exchanger, so the loop closes through the gas itself.
  4. Now wire the hot bed outlet into the exchanger hot side (HOT IN), and the hot side out to the tank.without this pipe the bed outlet never warms the feed, the bed stays asleep and the heater pays for everything.
  5. Pipe the exchanger's hot outlet on to the product tank: every outlet must end somewhere.the cooled bed gas has to leave the plant before it can be commissioned.
  6. Set the heater near 410 °C and commission.the catalyst wakes at about 410 °C, and the Arrhenius rate climbs like a cliff.
  7. Read the bed: inlet, hottest point, conversion.the heat of the reaction is what lights the rest of the bed once the front end wakes up.
  8. Open the exchanger card and raise its AREA knob (it starts at 10 m2; about 50 m2 lets the heater only trim to 10 kW or less), then commission again.fuel is the running cost. The more heat the exchanger returns, the less the heater burns.
  9. Ask Why? and read the approach to equilibrium.it uses your own bed temperatures.

What it teaches

Light-off is a cliff, not a slope. Your gas entered at … °C and left at … °C with … % converted, against … % that equilibrium allows at that outlet temperature. The heater burned … kW of fuel: heat from the bed, through the exchanger, is free. Runs tried: ….

L18 Exam Bridge: The Oxygen Rig

Emergency oxygen bench

Brief

  • The emergency oxygen generator decomposes peroxide: k is 1.15 × 10⁻³ per second. How long until 5 g becomes 3 g? Write it down first.
  • Then make the rig deliver five litres of oxygen in ten minutes at 25 °C, with no heating. One rig on the bench will; strip the rest. Two different ks: the exam sum uses the textbook k = 1.15 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, but the rigs have their own k at 25 °C, written on each rig's card with its catalyst: no catalyst 1 × 10⁻⁶, iodide 1 × 10⁻³, catalase 1 × 10⁻¹ s⁻¹. Five litres in ten minutes needs about 81 % of the peroxide gone, a k of at least 2.7 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. Money: everything on the bench counts as spent, and deleting a rig takes its price off the total again: a full refund.

Goal

  • At least … L of O₂ in ten minutes
  • The charge stays at … °C or colder, no heating

Stars

  1. Deliver 5 L of O₂ in ten minutes at 25 °C
  2. Measure the rate at 293 K and 313 K (two runs) and work out Ea
  3. Both, and Call it: the time for 5 g to become 3 g, within 0.5 %

Steps

  1. Three rigs stand on the bench, one per catalyst: none, iodide, catalase. Click each rig and read its catalyst and k on the card; keep the one whose k is at least 2.7 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ and take away the other two.a rig with nothing piped to it cannot be commissioned, and a catalyst only helps if it is in the line.
  2. Pipe the charge through the rig you kept and on to the emergency header.the burette reads the oxygen the rig delivers to the header.
  3. Commission and watch the burette.first order means the rate is proportional to what is left, so the column rises fast and then slows.
  4. Read the oxygen volume and the peroxide decomposed.the oxygen is half the peroxide that went, as an ideal gas at the rig temperature.
  5. Run the charge at 293 K, then at 313 K, and compare the rate constants.two temperatures give Ea in the Arrhenius two-point form, ln of the rate ratio over the change in 1/T.
  6. Ask Why? and read the half-life.the half-life of a first-order reaction is ln 2 over k.

What it teaches

A catalyst opens a lower-Ea road and the rate constant grows like exp(−Ea/RT). Your rig (catalyst: …) has k = … s⁻¹ and a half-life of … s, so … % of the peroxide went and the burette read … L at … °C. Runs tried: ….
