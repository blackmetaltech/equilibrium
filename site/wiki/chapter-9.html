L34 HAZOP Node

Acid plant, waste-heat cooler E-102

Brief

  • Cooler E-102 takes the hot converter gas and raises steam on its shell. Walk every guide word over every parameter and run the ones that could hurt.
  • For each deviation that matters write the cause, the consequence and a safeguard that really stops it. The engine runs each deviation on the node.
  • Given for the call-it: the gas cools against boiling water at the drum temperature (T sat on the header, from the drum pressure) as an effectiveness-NTU exchanger with NTU = 1.5. Losing feed water does not move the shell temperature until the level falls.

Goal

  • Rows for all … serious deviations (equipment damage, release, corrosion)
  • Every row has the right consequence, a credible cause and an effective safeguard

Stars

  1. Goal met: every serious deviation recorded and correct
  2. Call it: the gas outlet temperature just after the boiler feed water drops to 20 %, within 3 %
  3. Proposed safeguards at the cheapest set that covers every serious deviation

Steps

  1. Pick a cell of the sheet, for example gas flow and no, and run the deviation. Read the outlet temperature and the tube metal.a guide word is a question; the engine answers it with numbers from the node.
  2. Write your first row: cause, the consequence class the numbers point to, and a safeguard.a worksheet row is only worth the safeguard it names. A safeguard has to act on that deviation.
  3. Run the rest: the low and the dry-drum cases are where the damage is. Cover every serious one.the deviation nobody wrote down is the one that happens.
  4. Turn the key to grade the sheet.the sheet is checked against the simulated deviations, not against opinion.
  5. Ask Why? and compare the gas outlet with the shell temperature.the gas side limits the heat exchange, so cutting water does not change the outlet at first.

L37 The Analyser Lies

Control room, loop flow meters

Brief

  • Five flow meters on the loop and the mass balance does not close. One of them is drifting. Reconcile the data and find it.
  • Normalised adjustments tell the guilty meter from the honest ones. Call it from the three node imbalances before you reconcile: a wrong meter shows in every balance it sits on. Then flag one meter, which also takes it out of the balance. Taking a meter out removes one degree of freedom, so the chi-square limit tightens from 7.81 (three balances) to 5.99 (two): a smaller number has to be reached, which is why the goal limit changes after you flag.

Goal

  • The drifting meter flagged, and only that one
  • The balance closes: chi-square at most … (the 95 % limit for the balances left: 3, minus one for every meter taken out)
  • Call-outs at most …

Stars

  1. Goal met: right meter, balance closed
  2. Call it: which meter is drifting
  3. Reconciled flows as close to the truth as the reference

Steps

  1. Run the reconciliation and look at the three node imbalances.each node should balance to within the meter noise; a node that does not points at a meter on it.
  2. Flag the meter with the largest normalised adjustment (flagging also takes it out of the balance).a normalised adjustment above about three is a gross error, not noise.
  3. Take that meter out of the balance and look at the chi-square again.a balance with a biased meter in it spreads the error over all five.
  4. Turn the key to hand in the work order.the call-out is only judged against the truth when you commit.
  5. Ask Why? and read the mixer imbalance.the imbalance is the sum of three meter errors; the least squares shares it out by their noise.

What it teaches
