L34 HAZOP Node Classic look

Acid plant, waste-heat cooler E-102

Brief

  • Cooler E-102 takes the hot converter gas and raises steam on its shell. Walk every guide word over every parameter and run the ones that could hurt.
  • For each deviation that matters write the cause, the consequence and a safeguard that really stops it. The engine runs each deviation on the node.
  • Given for the call-it: the gas cools against boiling water at the drum temperature (T sat on the header, from the drum pressure) as an effectiveness-NTU exchanger with NTU = 1.5. Losing feed water does not move the shell temperature until the level falls. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • Rows for all 8 serious deviations (equipment damage, release, corrosion)
  • Every row has the right consequence, a credible cause and an effective safeguard

Stars

  1. Goal met: every serious deviation recorded and correct
  2. Call it: the gas outlet temperature just after the boiler feed water drops to 20 %, within 3 %
  3. Proposed safeguards at the cheapest set that covers every serious deviation

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Over budget.
  • A cause is not credible.
  • A consequence is wrongly classed.
  • A serious deviation is missing.
  • A safeguard does not act on its deviation.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Pick a cell of the sheet, for example gas flow and no, and run the deviation. Read the outlet temperature and the tube metal.a guide word is a question; the engine answers it with numbers from the node.
  2. Write your first row: cause, the consequence class the numbers point to, and a safeguard.a worksheet row is only worth the safeguard it names. A safeguard has to act on that deviation.
  3. Run the rest: the low and the dry-drum cases are where the damage is. Cover every serious one.the deviation nobody wrote down is the one that happens.
  4. Turn the key to grade the sheet.the sheet is checked against the simulated deviations, not against opinion.
  5. Ask Why? and compare the gas outlet with the shell temperature.the gas side limits the heat exchange, so cutting water does not change the outlet at first.

L35 Start-up Classic look

Ammonia loop, control room

Brief

  • The loop is cold, full of air, and the catalyst is not reduced. Write the start-up procedure from the cards, in order.
  • Oxygen below one percent before any hydrogen, heat no faster than fifty kelvin an hour, a tightness test, and cooling before the circulator. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • No hazard and no step refused
  • Catalyst reduced, loop at full pressure, cooling and circulation running

Stars

  1. Goal met: the procedure is safe and the loop is ready
  2. Call it: the minutes to heat the converter from 300 to 650 K at the ramp limit, within 5 %
  3. Start-up time at or below the reference procedure

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • A card was refused.
  • Over budget.
  • Hydrogen met oxygen.
  • The loop is not ready.
  • Hydrogen was released or nearly so.
  • Thermal shock.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Put the cards in the order you would do them. The procedure runs from the first card to the last.every card has a precondition. A card done too early is refused, or it does harm.
  2. Check the consequence column after each change: refused cards and hazards show there.the oxygen, the temperature and the pressure are carried from card to card; the hazard is read from them.
  3. Turn the key to run the procedure.a procedure is only safe if it was run, not if it reads well.
  4. Ask Why? and read the heating time against the ramp limit.the ramp limit protects the catalyst and the refractory; it fixes the time.

L36 Permit to Work Classic look

Exchanger E-103, permit office

Brief

  • E-103 is fouled and has to be opened while the plant runs on the bypass. Write the isolation and the job in order.
  • Two blocks and a bleed, or a blind. The outlet pair has a valve that passes: read the bleed. Every hour out of service costs money, every skipped check costs more.
  • Given for the call-it: every hour the exchanger is out of service the plant loses 1800 USD on the bypass. The minutes of each step are printed on its card. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • Exchanger cleaned and back in service

Stars

  2. Call it: the bypass loss of the shortest safe job, within 5 %
  3. Bypass loss at or below the reference permit

If it fails

  • Over budget.
  • Opened with gas present.
  • Worked on a live line.

Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Start with the isolation: close the blocks on both sides, then add the rest of the steps in order.the plant stays live on both sides of the exchanger until a barrier is closed and proved.

  3. Turn the key to run the permit.the permit is judged by what the exchanger does when it is opened.
  4. Ask Why? and read the loss against the hours.the bypass loss is the rate times the time out of service.

L37 The Analyser Lies Classic look

Control room, loop flow meters

Brief

  • Five flow meters on the loop and the mass balance does not close. One of them is drifting. Reconcile the data and find it.
  • Normalised adjustments tell the guilty meter from the honest ones. Call it from the three node imbalances before you reconcile: a wrong meter shows in every balance it sits on. Then flag one meter, which also takes it out of the balance. Taking a meter out removes one degree of freedom, so the chi-square limit tightens from 7.81 (three balances) to 5.99 (two): a smaller number has to be reached, which is why the goal limit changes after you flag. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • The drifting meter flagged, and only that one
  • The balance closes: chi-square at most 7.81 (the 95 % limit for the balances left: 3, minus one for every meter taken out)

Stars

  1. Goal met: right meter, balance closed
  2. Call it: which meter is drifting
  3. Reconciled flows as close to the truth as the reference

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Over budget.
  • No meter flagged.
  • The balance does not close.
  • The wrong meter.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Run the reconciliation and look at the three node imbalances.each node should balance to within the meter noise; a node that does not points at a meter on it.
  2. Flag the meter with the largest normalised adjustment (flagging also takes it out of the balance).a normalised adjustment above about three is a gross error, not noise.
  3. Take that meter out of the balance and look at the chi-square again.a balance with a biased meter in it spreads the error over all five.
  4. Turn the key to hand in the work order.the call-out is only judged against the truth when you commit.
  5. Ask Why? and read the mixer imbalance.the imbalance is the sum of three meter errors; the least squares shares it out by their noise.

L38 Turnaround Plan Classic look

Planning office, 20-day window

Brief

  • Eight jobs, one crane, eight crew and a twenty day window. The jobs below are in the order the trades asked for, and as listed the plan runs past the window and over budget on lost margin. That is where you start, and putting it right is your job.
  • Move the jobs into the order that keeps crews and crane busy on the longest chain. A second crane can be hired, at a price.
  • The critical path assumes unlimited crews and cranes. With one crane and eight crew the best order is 18 days; a second crane (hire 450,000 USD) allows 16 days, which saves 660,000 USD of lost margin, so it nets 210,000 USD only if your plan really reaches 16 days: at 17 days the crane costs more than it saves. The crew bars under the plan show the crew in use each day against the limit of 8. The call-it is the critical path, not the days you can reach. Call it: predict before your first commission. The answer box locks when you commission; a right call earns a star.

Goal

  • Plant back in 20 days or fewer

Stars

  1. Goal met: back inside the window
  2. Call it: the shortest the turnaround could be with unlimited crews and cranes, within 5 %
  3. Cost at or below the reference plan

If it fails

The printout names one of these when a goal is missed:

  • Over budget.
  • Past the window.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Find the critical path first: the jobs with no float. Then move jobs on the board.any delay on the critical path is a delay of the whole turnaround; float is room to wait.
  2. Two jobs want the crane. Put the one on the critical path first, or hire a second crane.one crane serves one job at a time, so crane jobs queue behind each other.
  3. Turn the key to lock the plan.a plan is judged by the days the plant stays down.
  4. Ask Why? and compare your days with the critical path.the difference is the cost of the limited crews and crane.
